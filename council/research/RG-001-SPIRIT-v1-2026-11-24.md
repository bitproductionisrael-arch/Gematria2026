## 1. Historical mapping, primary text
Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa, De occulta philosophia libri tres (1533), book II, chapter XX, describes three classes: “unitatum, secunda denariorum, tertia centenariorum” (Latin text; units, tens, hundreds). He distinguishes consonantal i/u and aspirated hi/hu to complete 27 signs. The 1651 English translation gives the table A–I=1–9, K–S=10–90, T=100, vocalic V=200, X=300, Y=400, Z=500, consonantal I=600, consonantal V=700, HI=800, HV=900. The modern 26-letter table in calculator.html (AGR, line 691 at reviewed base) maps U=200, J=600, V=700, W=900 and has no separate HI=800. EN_ORD is A1Z26 (lines 643–644). Thus the modern engine alias Jewish Gematria is an Agrippa-derived adaptation, not traditional Hebrew gematria and not an exact transcription of all 27 historical signs. The consonantal V=700 in the public example has explicit historical support; changing the sign/function or cipher changes the numerical result. This is textual provenance, not evidence of supernatural causation. The author himself distinguishes names given through varying national customs from the birth names he considered astrologically significant (end of II.XX); his divination claims are historical beliefs, not empirical evidence. A 1533 edition catalog and Latin text transcription were consulted; no direct image collation of the original page was completed.

## 2. Literal language control
Under the fixed maps (Agrippa-derived J, Simple S), word-level pairs and differences are AMIT 140/43/97, MIKE 54/38/16, ROV 830/55/775. Hebrew standard word totals are 520/160/208. The equality is at the full-name level, not word-by-word correspondence. V contributes 700−22=678 of 888. A–I have J=S; the four zero-difference positions are A,I,I,E. The rest are M,T,M,K,R,O,V. MIKE and MIIK preserve the difference but not both totals (1028/140 for the full MIIK variant); this is an existing observation, not a new finding. ROB and ROU are formal spelling controls, not claims about a person's actual surname: full Latin sums 326/116 and 524/135, respectively. Hebrew unpointed bet may represent /b/ or /v/; phonology must be independently established, not inferred from the desired sum. The comparison is sensitive to orthographic choices and must distinguish legitimate transliteration variants from arbitrary string mutations.

## 3. Fixed-position control (new proposal/result)
Keep the seven nonzero-difference positions and word boundaries fixed. Replace each of the four positions with any letter A–I. There are 9^4=6561 distinct strings; all preserve J−S=888. The fixed seven positions sum to 112 under S; the four replacements must sum to 24 to preserve S=136. Exact enumeration gives 375 strings, including the original, with J=1024 and S=136 (374 alternatives). The independently checkable inclusion-exclusion count is C(23,3)−4*C(14,3)+6*C(5,3)=375. This is a constructed string-control population, not a natural-name frequency or proof of equivalent Hebrew readings. The corpus already records eight distinct 11-letter matches from a selected Gematrix dataset and a uniform-letter model; neither is a random real-name sample. English ×6 provides no independent evidence.

## 4. Proposed next test, for MATTER
Verify the counts and scope independently; then preregister a natural-name and cross-script control with fixed normalization, pronunciation/transliteration rules, sampled name population, and correction for multiple variants. Report both all hits and misses, and distinguish exact pair, difference only and Hebrew match. Do not multiply marginal rates as if transliteration were independent. No claim of uniqueness or probability without a defined population. Keep the D4 geometry as a separately proved identity, not another independently sampled observation of a name.

## 5. Proposed conclusion RG-001 v1
For the fixed publicly documented spelling and the exact current maps, the equality is reproducible. Agrippa's historical Latin consonantal assignment supports the provenance of the map but the 26-letter online variant is an adaptation; the two Latin totals share their input and mappings, and transliteration couples the Hebrew input. A difference or pair collision alone does not establish uniqueness, empirical rarity or any personal/metaphysical identity. Further empirical rarity testing remains open under RG-005.
