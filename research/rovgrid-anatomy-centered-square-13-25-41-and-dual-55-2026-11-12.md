## Result 1: genuinely nested bone sets 13 → 25 → 41
Work with **distinct adult bone identities**, fixed bilateral labels, and separate bone categories.

C = {12 thoracic vertebrae T1..T12, 1 sternum} =13.
L = {12 left ribs L1..L12} =12.
R = {12 right ribs R1..R12} =12.
G = {left clavicle, left scapula, right clavicle, right scapula} =4.

Define three *nested* bone sets:
A3=C, cardinality 13.
A4=C∪L, cardinality 13+12=25.
A5=C∪L∪R∪G, cardinality 13+12+12+4=41.

These are exactly three successive **centered square numbers** Q(n)=n²+(n−1)², indexed n=3,4,5:
Q3=3²+2²=9+4=13;
Q4=4²+3²=16+9=25;
Q5=5²+4²=25+16=41.
Increments are 12 and 16, as required by the general centered-square pattern.
Earlier 1 and 5 are also numbers of this mathematical sequence but are not part of this same A3→A4→A5 nesting; do not fabricate a natural physical 1→5→13 nesting from this result.

**Important:** The 25 bones in A4 are *not* the canonical bilateral thoracic cage of 25 (24 ribs+sternum). A4 consists of the 13 centrally defined bones plus **only the twelve left ribs**. Both inventories have 25 members but different membership, one of which includes the 12 thoracic vertebrae. This distinction is essential for animation and any empirical interpretation.

Like all earlier dot-based geometry, the 13,25,41 actual bones are moved to positions in abstract centered-square diagrams. The body does not naturally position those bones as perfect centered squares.
